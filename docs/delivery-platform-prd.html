Product requirements document

A multi-vertical delivery platform for Qatar

Food, grocery, pharmacy and parcel delivery on one platform, operated with an employed rider fleet. This document defines what will be built, in what order, and how we will know it is done.

Summary

What we are building and why it is scoped this way.

The platform lets customers order from restaurants, supermarkets and pharmacies, or send a parcel from one address to another, and have it delivered by the client's own riders. It consists of four applications and one shared backend.

Platforms of this kind succeed or fail on operations, not features. This scope is therefore designed to prove one delivery zone end to end with a small set of merchants and riders, then expand. Every business rule (fees, zones, commissions, opening hours) is configurable by the client's team without a developer.

Design principle. Build one generic model that all four verticals share, rather than four separate products. A pharmacy order and a restaurant order follow the same lifecycle; only the catalog attributes and approval steps differ.

Goals and non-goals

Agreeing what is out of scope is as important as what is in.

Goals for the first release

  • A customer can find a store, order, pay by card or cash, and watch the rider approach on a map.
  • A merchant can manage its catalog and accept, prepare and hand over orders from a phone.
  • A rider receives assignments automatically, navigates, and confirms delivery with proof.
  • The operations team can see every live order and rider, intervene manually, and settle cash daily.
  • Money is fully accounted for: every payment, refund, merchant payable and rider cash collection is in one ledger.
  • Everything is bilingual, English and Arabic, with right-to-left layout.

Explicitly not in the first release

  • Order batching (one rider carrying several orders).
  • Surge or dynamic delivery pricing.
  • Customer subscriptions (free-delivery membership).
  • Freelance rider onboarding, since the fleet is employed.
  • Integration with merchants' own POS systems.
  • Dark stores or client-owned inventory.
  • Loyalty points and referral programmes.

These are candidates for later phases and are listed in Delivery phases.

Users and applications

Four applications, one backend, four kinds of user.

Customer app

iOS, Android and mobile web. Anyone in Qatar with a phone number.

  • Browse, search, order, pay
  • Live tracking and notifications
  • Addresses, wallet, order history

Merchant app

Android and iOS tablet or phone. Store owners and counter staff.

  • Catalog and stock
  • Accept, prepare, hand over
  • Sales and payout reports

Rider app

Android first. Employed riders on shift.

  • Clock in, receive tasks
  • Navigate, pick up, deliver
  • Cash in hand and earnings

Operations console

Web. Dispatchers, fleet managers, finance and admins.

  • Live map and manual dispatch
  • Merchants, zones, fees, promos
  • Settlement, refunds, reports

Customer app

Functional scope for the first release.

Account and addresses

  • Sign in with Qatar mobile number and one-time code; guest browsing allowed.
  • Saved addresses with map pin, building, zone and street numbers, and delivery notes.
  • Language switch between English and Arabic at any time.

Discovery

  • Home screen per vertical, with stores filtered to the customer's delivery zone.
  • Store list with open or closed state, delivery fee, estimated time and rating.
  • Search across stores and products; category filters; promoted placements set by operations.

Ordering

  • Single-store cart; modifiers and notes per item; grocery quantity by weight or unit.
  • Checkout shows itemised fees: subtotal, delivery, service fee, discount, total. Structure is VAT-ready.
  • Payment by card through a Qatar-licensed gateway, by cash on delivery, or from wallet balance.
  • Promo codes with rules: minimum order, first order only, vertical, expiry, usage cap.
  • Pharmacy: prescription photo upload where an item requires it; order waits for pharmacist approval.
  • Parcel: pickup and drop-off addresses, receiver phone, size class, optional cash-on-delivery amount.

After ordering

  • Status timeline with push notifications at each step.
  • Live rider location on map once picked up, with estimated arrival.
  • Call or chat with the rider through the app without exposing personal numbers.
  • Cancel free of charge until the merchant accepts; fee rules after that are configurable.
  • Rate the order and the rider; report a problem and request a refund.

Merchant app

What a store needs to serve orders without a laptop.

  • Incoming order alert with sound; accept or reject with reason within a configurable window.
  • Set preparation time per order; mark ready for pickup.
  • Catalog: categories, products in English and Arabic, photos, prices, modifiers, availability toggle.
  • Grocery and pharmacy: stock quantities, mark out of stock, propose substitutions to the customer.
  • Pharmacy: review uploaded prescription, approve or decline, add pharmacist note.
  • Store status: open, closed, busy (pauses new orders); weekly hours and holiday closures.
  • Reports: orders, revenue, commission, net payable, downloadable statement per period.
  • Staff accounts with restricted permissions.

Rider app

Designed for an employed fleet, where tasks are assigned rather than offered.

  • Clock in and out, with a geofence check at the assigned hub or zone.
  • Status: available, on task, on break. Shift schedule visible for the week.
  • Assigned task appears with store, customer area, and expected pickup time; rider acknowledges within 60 seconds or operations is alerted.
  • Navigation to store, then to customer, handing off to Google Maps. Location shared every few seconds while on task.
  • Pickup confirmation; delivery confirmation with photo and, where required, identity or one-time code from the customer.
  • Cash collection recorded per order; running cash-in-hand total; end-of-shift settlement summary.
  • Earnings and performance summary: deliveries, on-time percentage, ratings.
  • Works with poor connectivity: queues status updates and syncs when back online.

Operations console

The control room. This is where the business is actually run.

Live operations

  • Map of all riders and live orders, colour-coded by status and delay.
  • Order board with filters; open any order to see its full timeline and contacts.
  • Override dispatch: assign, reassign, or hold; cancel with refund; contact customer, merchant or rider.
  • Alerts: unacknowledged assignments, orders past expected time, riders idle or offline mid-shift.

Fleet management

  • Rider records: employment details, documents, vehicle, zone, licence expiry reminders.
  • Shift roster and attendance; leave and break rules.
  • Cash settlement: daily reconciliation of cash collected against delivered cash orders, per rider.
  • Performance dashboard per rider and per zone.

Configuration

  • Zones drawn on a map, each with delivery fee rules, minimum order, and rider pool.
  • Merchant onboarding, commission rates, payout schedule, and approval of catalog changes if desired.
  • Service fees, cancellation policy, promo codes, banners and featured stores.
  • Notification templates in both languages.

Finance

  • Ledger view of every transaction; refunds and adjustments with approval trail.
  • Merchant payout statements; export to Excel and PDF.
  • Revenue, order volume and vertical mix reports by day, zone and merchant.

Dispatch rules

How riders are chosen. Simple and transparent first; smarter later.

When an order is accepted, the system calculates a target pickup time from the merchant's preparation estimate. Shortly before that time it selects a rider from the zone's pool using a score:

  • Estimated travel time from the rider's current position to the store, from the maps provider.
  • A penalty if the rider is finishing another delivery, based on remaining time on that task.
  • Exclusions: riders on break, offline, outside the zone, or with cash in hand above a configurable limit.

The lowest score wins and is assigned automatically. The rider must acknowledge within 60 seconds; otherwise the next rider is assigned and the dispatcher is alerted. Dispatchers can override any assignment from the console.

Why not batching yet. Carrying two orders at once roughly halves delivery cost but needs reliable preparation-time data and enough order density to work. Both come only after a few months of live operation, so it is planned for phase two.

Payments and money

Every riyal is traceable.

Payment methods

  • Card payments through a Qatar Central Bank-licensed gateway, with saved cards and Apple Pay or Google Pay where the gateway supports them. Card data never touches the platform.
  • Cash on delivery, recorded by the rider at handover.
  • Customer wallet, credited from refunds or promotional credit, spendable at checkout.

Ledger

A single double-entry ledger records: customer payments, gateway fees, merchant sales and commission, delivery and service fees, rider cash collected and settled, refunds and adjustments. From it the system produces merchant payout statements, rider cash settlements, and the client's revenue reports, all reconciling to the same source.

Refunds

  • Full or partial, to original payment method or to wallet; requires a reason and, above a configurable amount, a second approval.
  • Merchant-caused issues can be charged back against the merchant's payable.

Delivery phases

Each phase ends with a working, testable release. Durations assume prompt client feedback and merchant content readiness.

  1. Discovery and design

    Weeks 1 to 2

    Confirm this document, finalise fee and zone rules, select the payment gateway, agree branding. Deliverable: signed scope, clickable designs of key screens.

  2. Foundation

    Weeks 3 to 5

    Backend, data model, sign-in, zones, and the console's merchant and catalog management. Deliverable: the client team can onboard real merchants.

  3. Ordering

    Weeks 6 to 8

    Customer app browse-to-checkout with cash payment; merchant app order handling. Deliverable: an order can be placed and accepted end to end.

  4. Fleet and dispatch

    Weeks 9 to 11

    Rider app, automatic dispatch, live tracking, console live map with overrides, shifts and cash settlement. Deliverable: full delivery loop in one test zone.

  5. Money and verticals

    Weeks 12 to 13

    Card payments, wallet, refunds, ledger and statements; pharmacy approval flow; parcel flow; grocery substitutions. Deliverable: all four verticals operable.

  6. Hardening and launch

    Weeks 14 to 16

    Ratings, promotions, notification polish, load testing, app store submission, staff training, pilot with a limited merchant set. Deliverable: public launch in the first zone.

After launch

  • Phase 7: order batching, scheduled slots for food, richer promotions, merchant-facing analytics.
  • Phase 8: subscriptions, surge pricing, merchant POS integrations, additional cities and zones.

Assumptions and open questions

Items that affect scope, cost or timeline and need the client's answer.

Assumptions

  • The client supplies merchant contracts, catalog content and photos, and the rider workforce.
  • The client holds or will obtain the required commercial and delivery licences in Qatar.
  • Apple and Google developer accounts are in the client's name.
  • Third-party running costs (maps, SMS, hosting, gateway fees) are paid by the client directly.

Open questions

  • Preferred payment gateway, or should we evaluate options and recommend one?
  • Launch zone and expected merchant count at launch?
  • Should merchants be able to edit their own catalog freely, or does operations approve changes?
  • Cash-in-hand limit per rider and settlement frequency?
  • Will the client's brand exist before design starts, or is branding part of this engagement?
  • Is customer support handled in-app (chat) or by phone in the first release?

Acceptance criteria

The first release is complete when each of these can be demonstrated on real devices.

  1. A new customer signs up, orders from a restaurant, pays by card, and receives the order with live tracking throughout.
  2. A grocery order with an out-of-stock item is resolved by substitution agreed with the customer.
  3. A pharmacy order with a prescription is held, approved by the pharmacy, and delivered with identity check.
  4. A parcel is sent between two addresses with cash collected from the receiver and recorded.
  5. A rider clocks in, is auto-assigned three consecutive orders, and settles cash at end of shift with the totals matching the ledger.
  6. A dispatcher reassigns a delayed order to another rider from the live map.
  7. Finance issues a partial refund and generates a merchant payout statement that reconciles with the ledger.
  8. Every screen of every app is reviewed in Arabic with correct right-to-left layout.
  9. The platform sustains a load test at the agreed concurrency without errors.
